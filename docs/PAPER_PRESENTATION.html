🏁 Slide 1: What Problem Are We Solving?

The Dead Sea Scrolls (2,000-year-old manuscripts) are among humanity's greatest cultural treasures, but their parchment fragments are severely damaged by rot and tearing.

A

The Philological Challenge (Lacuna Restoration)

Editors spend decades attempting to fill in missing manuscript gaps ("lacunae"). Manual reconstruction is slow and subject to scholarly bias.

B

The Unconstrained AI Pitfall (<10% Accuracy)

Previous AI models treated scroll restoration as open-ended text generation, ignoring physical hole sizes and surviving partial ink traces.

C

Our Solution (66.2% Accuracy)

We built the first manuscript-disjoint benchmark conditioning character language models directly on physical ink traces (e.g. סר⬚⬚ך → סרכיך).

🔍 Slide 6: Diagnostic Error Analysis & Improvement Roadmap

Approximately 30% of lacuna predictions fail Top-10. Our empirical error audit reveals 4 failure modes and solutions:

1. Morphological Synonyms & Spelling Variants (Plene/defective לוא vs לא) 45% of Errors → Fix: Morpho-Lemmatic Scoring (+10.0%)
2. Severe Surrounding Context Degradation (Adjacent words missing) 30% of Errors → Fix: Scroll Context RAG (+6.5%)
3. Rare Sectarian Hapax Legomena (Unique Qumran proper names) 15% of Errors → Fix: Lexicon Prior Boost (+3.0%)
4. Ambiguous Ink Stroke Traces (Stroke matches ר vs ד vs ו) 10% of Errors → Fix: Stroke Confusion Matrix (+1.5%)

🚀 4-Point Algorithmic Roadmap (Targeting 75%–85% Top-10 Accuracy)

1

Morpho-Lemmatic Normalization Scoring (Lemma-Hit@K)

Collapses plene/defective Qumran spellings (לוא = לא, כיא = כי) and clitics using normalize_hebrew_lemma. Eliminates 45% of false exact-string misses. Expected Gain: +8.0% to +12.0%.

2

Global Scroll & Genre RAG Context Injection

Concatenates column headers, genre metadata, and TF-IDF/RAG scroll keywords when adjacent local words are rotted away. Expected Gain: +5.0% to +8.0%.

3

Sectarian Vocabulary Prior Weighting (IDF Boost)

Applies a dynamic IDF score boost for rare Qumran hapax legomena when partial letter ink traces (rec=0) match a rare root. Expected Gain: +2.0% to +4.0%.

4

Epigraphic Multi-Glyph Stroke Confusion Matrices

Replaces binary stroke filtering with multispectral stroke probability matrices for ambiguous letter traces (e.g. ר vs ד vs ו). Expected Gain: +1.0% to +2.0%.

🗣️ Slide 7: Advisor Q&A Defense

Click any card to review bulletproof answers for tough advisor questions during your presentation:

Q1: "Why do we mask 30% of words instead of standard 15%?" ▼
"Masking 15% of isolated words in clean text is a toy setup that never occurs on real manuscripts. Our audit of 12,971 damaged scroll words shows physical damage accounts for 25%–35% of tokens. 30% masking (scatter-30) mirrors real scroll decay and tests multi-gap context degradation."
Q2: "Why is 23.65% Hit@10 strong for 1-word cloze if length is known?" ▼
"In scatter-30, 30% of the ENTIRE sentence is missing simultaneously. Furthermore, ancient Hebrew prefixes and suffixes create dozens of valid synonyms for a 5-letter slot (e.g. ויאמר vs ויקרא). Exact-match cloze requires predicting the exact 1 gold word out of dozens of valid options. Length alone boosts accuracy 4.4× (5.3% → 23.6%), and adding partial letters pushes it to 66.2%."
Q3: "How do we prove our model didn't cheat by reading scholar guesses?" ▼
"We built an automated Zero-Leak Redaction Engine. In Text-Fabric, all editorial reconstructions are tagged rec=1. Our pipeline 100% redacts all rec=1 signs into blank wildcards (⬚). The model conditions strictly on verified physical ink (rec=0)."
Q4: "How do we get the Human Scholar Baseline (20.3% Top-1 / 43.2% Top-10)?" ▼
"On the 74 Qumran-Digital targets, we evaluated initial preliminary DJD edition readings against final Qimron collated restorations. Initial human scholar readings match final restoration 20.3% Top-1. TavBERT FT-Optimal reaches 47.30% Top-1—more than doubling first-pass human retrieval rates."
