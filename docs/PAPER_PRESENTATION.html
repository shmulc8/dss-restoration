🏁 Slide 1: What Problem Are We Solving?

The Dead Sea Scrolls are 2,000-year-old manuscripts whose parchment has decayed and torn, leaving gaps ("lacunae") in the text. Editors fill these gaps by hand, over decades.

A

The Philological Task (Lacuna Restoration)

Reconstructing a missing word is slow, and competing proposals by different editors are common — the Qumran-Digital variant database records 1,811 published reading proposals for the sites we sampled.

B

Unconstrained Language Modeling Is Weak Here pilot

Asked to fill a real lacuna from context alone (regime U0), a fine-tuned MLM places an attributed scholarly reading in its Top-10 for 9.5% of targets (n=74).

C

Physical Evidence Is What Moves the Needle pilot

Conditioning the same model on the surviving ink of the damaged word plus an approximate gap length (regime P0, ±1 char) raises Top-10 to 63.5% on the same 74 targets — a +54.0 pp gain. This measures the value of the physical evidence supplied to the decoder, not an improvement in the language model.

Why the physical channel exists at all

Across the 27,814 parsed lacunae, 85.0% contain at least one damaged word that still shows ink, and 29.7% of individual damaged words carry a partial trace (49,130 / 165,239). Roughly seven in ten damaged words show nothing — the P0 regime simply does not apply to them, which bounds how far this approach can reach. data/derived/nonbib_lacunae.jsonl

⚠️ Open issue: two split registries coexist

Two incompatible split definitions exist in the repository: dss_scroll_splits_v1.json (canonical, 732 scrolls, 531/108/93) and the corpus manifest's scroll_splits (736 scrolls, 413 train / 103 dev / 220 heldout). The QD real-lacuna benchmark was run against the manifest registry, so 40 of its 74 targets fall in the canonical registry's train split. Slide 5, Table 2 reports the canonical-clean subsets alongside the headline figure. This must be consolidated before submission. Separately, only 374 of the 732 registry scrolls yield a text chunk at all — the rest are too fragmentary to reach the 20-preserved-word minimum, and a further 3 chunk-bearing scrolls are absent from the registry entirely.

🔍 Slide 6: Measured Diagnostics & Roadmap

Context degradation stress test measured

Real lacunae rarely sit in clean text — the neighbouring words are usually damaged too. This ablation randomly masks a fraction of surrounding context words on 120 held-out cases (≤30 per gap-length bucket) and measures the effect. analysis/reports/context_noise_ablation.md

Context noise Slot-level Top-1 Sequence-level Top-1
0% (clean context)12.2%7.5%
10%11.9%8.3%
25%8.7%5.8%
40%7.4%2.5%

Between 0% and 40% noise, slot accuracy falls by ~40% relative and sequence accuracy by ~67%. Context degradation is therefore a measured, first-order limitation — which is exactly why the physical ink channel matters, since it survives when the context does not.

Where the remaining errors come from not yet quantified

Under the P0 baseline, 36.5% of the 74 real-lacuna targets miss Top-10. The failure categories below were identified by manual inspection of held-out misses. The per-category shares have not been measured — the diagnostic CSV that once backed them is no longer in the repository, so no percentages are claimed here. Re-deriving this taxonomy on the current model is a prerequisite for the error-analysis section of the paper.

1. Orthographic and morphological variants (plene/defective לוא vs לא; inflection as in אשמות vs אשמים) share unmeasured
2. Severe context degradation (adjacent words also missing) — confirmed as first-order by the ablation above share unmeasured
3. Rare sectarian vocabulary and hapax legomena share unmeasured
4. Ambiguous ink traces — several real words fit the surviving strokes (e.g. ⬚⬚לעים → קלעים / סלעים / מבלעים) share unmeasured

🚀 Four-point roadmap — implemented, not yet evaluated

⚠️ No accuracy numbers are claimed for any of these

All four components exist in eval/candidate_generator.py and eval/metrics_runner.py with unit tests, but none has been run against a benchmark, so no accuracy is attributable to any of them. Each is described below by what the code actually does, so that the evaluation can be designed against reality — two of the four need reworking before they could support a published claim at all.

1

Lemma-normalised scoring (normalize_hebrew_lemma())

Collapses final letter forms, common Qumran plene spellings (לוא→לא, כיא→כי), affixed particles and some suffixes. This relaxes the metric, it does not improve the model — it must be reported as a secondary "lemma-level agreement" figure alongside exact match, never as accuracy.

2

Retrieval-augmented context injection

Adds column headers, Pesher citation formulas (פשרו על) and retrieved scroll keywords when the local context is destroyed. Note that the one retrieval variant already measured on real lacunae produced a 0.0 pp change (Table 2), so a positive result here cannot be assumed.

3

Sectarian vocabulary prior (SectarianIDFBooster)

What it actually is: a hand-written lexicon of 15 terms (סרך, משכיל, יחד, …) with hand-assigned weights from 2.0 to 3.5, matched by substring. It computes no inverse document frequency and reads no corpus counts, so calling it "IDF" is inaccurate. Before it can be used in a paper it should be replaced with genuine corpus-derived IDF over the training split, and its lexicon must not overlap the evaluation targets.

4

Stroke-confusion matching (EpigraphicStrokeFilter)

What it actually is: four hand-written letter groups and a single similarity constant of 0.85, applied with a 0.85 threshold — so any substitution within a group passes the filter unconditionally. It is a relaxed matcher, not a probability matrix, and it will admit readings that the surviving ink excludes. The group {ר ד ו ן נ י} collapses six letters; the pairs {מ ס} and {ב כ} are hard to defend palaeographically for the relevant hands. A defensible version needs per-hand confusion probabilities estimated from a labelled palaeographic sample, and any gain must be reported as relaxed-match agreement.

🗣️ Slide 8: Advisor Q&A

Click any card. Answers state what is measured and what is not.

Q1: "Why mask 30% of words instead of the standard 15%?" ▼
Masking 15% of isolated words in clean text does not resemble a damaged manuscript. In the parsed corpus, damaged positions outnumber preserved ones — 165,239 gap words against 103,355 preserved words — so 30% simultaneous masking is if anything conservative. It also stresses the multi-gap regime, where the words neighbouring a lacuna are themselves missing; the context ablation on Slide 6 shows that this is where accuracy actually degrades.
Q2: "Is ~21% Hit@10 on single-word cloze actually good?" ▼
It is weak in absolute terms, and we should present it as a diagnostic rather than a headline. Two things make the task hard: in scatter-30 roughly a third of the sentence is missing at once, and Hebrew morphology means many inflections fit a slot of a given length, while exact match credits only the one gold string. But we should resist inflating it — no model in Table 1 is statistically separated from its neighbour, and fine-tuning does not beat the base checkpoint. The result worth defending is not the cloze number; it is the +54.0 pp effect of physical conditioning on real lacunae.
Q3: "How do you know the model isn't reading the editors' guesses?" ▼
Text-Fabric flags every editorial reconstruction as rec=1 on the sign. The corpus builder never emits that text — modern_reconstruction_text_emitted: false in the manifest — so both training and inference see only rec=0 physical ink, with gap sizes retained as metadata. Fine-tuning data contains no square-bracket restorations. For Table 2 the reference readings come from a separate source (Qumran-Digital publication rows), not from the input text.
Q4: "What exactly is the human comparison in Table 2?" ▼
It is Qumran-Digital's own recorded initial reading for each target, scored against the same set of attributed restorations: 20.3% Top-1, 43.2% Top-10. Three cautions. It is a database field, not a controlled human study and not a comparison of one edition against another. Because QD catalogues disputed sites, a low score for the initial reading is partly definitional. And it is available for only some targets. It bounds the difficulty of these particular sites; it does not license a claim that the model outperforms scholars.
Q5: "Can you restore multi-word gaps of unknown length?" ▼
Not yet. With the word-slot count supplied, whole-gap sequence accuracy is 7.0% Top-10 (9.0% with retrieval) on 100 held-out spans, and on the synthetic-span diagnostic every system scores 0% on two- and three-word spans. LengthEnsembleCharMLMGenerator is implemented and unit-tested but has never been benchmarked, so we claim no number for it. docs/RESULTS.md lists unknown-length multiword restoration as unsupported, and that is accurate.
Q6: "Which of these numbers would survive peer review?" ▼
Defensible as pilots, with their limitations stated: the U0 → P0 contrast on 74 real lacunae (9.5% → 63.5% Top-10, bootstrap 51.4–74.3%); the cloze table at n=729 with its overlapping intervals; the WordPiece alignment failure (279/729, 38.3%); Pesher source recovery (86.6% / 99.1%) with its permutation control; the corpus statistics; and the split construction.

Not yet publishable: anything about the four roadmap components, unknown-length multi-word restoration, a retrieval benefit to restoration, per-category error shares, and any model accuracy at the 3,695-lacuna scale.

Blocking issues before submission: consolidate the two split registries and re-run Table 2 on one of them; recompute the MsBERT bootstrap intervals on the all-words denominator; run the character models on the QD benchmark so the flagship result is not MsBERT-only; run mcnemar_test() on the paired predictions so that model comparisons carry a test rather than a bare point estimate; and grow n beyond 74 (12 on the strictest subset). Per docs/RESULTS.md, no result in this project is yet a frozen paper result.
Q7: "Why do published editions sometimes disagree about which letters survive?" ▼
Early editors read faint traces on torn edges under optical magnification, and later collations with better imaging sometimes revise them — a faint downstroke read as ד may be re-read as ר. This is a real palaeographic phenomenon and it is why Qumran-Digital records multiple attributed readings per site, which is exactly what Table 2 scores against. It is not, however, a justification for the current EpigraphicStrokeFilter: a blanket 0.85 pass within hand-written letter groups discards genuine evidence rather than modelling uncertainty. Quantifying letter confusion properly, per scribal hand, is future work.
