\begin{abstract}
Restoring missing text in 2,000-year-old Dead Sea Scrolls (\textit{lacunae}) is a fundamental challenge in biblical studies and digital humanities. Drawing inspiration from recent pioneering applications of Masked Language Modeling (MLM) to cuneiform tablets (\cite{lazar2021filling}), ancient Hebrew inscriptions (\cite{fono2024embible}), and medieval Hebrew manuscripts (\cite{shmidman2024msbert}), we present a comprehensive epigraphic restoration framework specifically engineered for non-biblical Dead Sea Scrolls. We demonstrate that subword tokenization (WordPiece) suffers a \textbf{38.3\% token alignment drop rate} on ancient character-bounded gaps, whereas character-level MLMs (TavBERT FT) eliminate alignment drops entirely (\textbf{0.0\% drops}), achieving \textbf{23.65\% Hit@10} on synthetic cloze (\texttt{scatter-30}, $n=729$). On real physical manuscript lacunae, conditioning on surviving ink traces ($P0$) and physical hole bounds ($L \pm 1$) lifts Top-10 restoration accuracy from 9.46\% ($U0$ unconstrained) to 64.86\% ($P0$). Incorporating our 4-point algorithmic roadmap (morpho-lemma normalization, quote-aware Pesher RAG, IDF term boosting, and epigraphic stroke filtering) elevates accuracy to \textbf{66.22\% Top-1 / 83.78\% Top-10 / 87.84\% Top-20} on peer-reviewed Qumran-Digital scholar targets ($n=74$), more than tripling initial 1950s human scholar editions (\textbf{20.27\% Top-1}). Finally, for unknown-length multi-word lacunae ($L \in [3, 15]$), our length-ensemble beam search (\texttt{LengthEnsembleCharMLMGenerator}) breaks the 0.0\% structural collapse wall of standard single-length MLMs, achieving \textbf{14.2\% Hit@10 / 14.5\% slot accuracy}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Dead Sea Scrolls—discovered in eleven caves near Qumran between 1947 and 1956—represent the most significant manuscript discovery of the twentieth century, containing over 25,000 fragments of ancient Hebrew and Aramaic texts dated between the 3rd century BCE and the 1st century CE. Over two millennia of physical deterioration, moisture, and parchment rot have left extensive holes (\textit{lacunae}) across these scroll fragments.

\subsection{Lineage and Related Work}

Recent advances in neural natural language processing have formulated ancient text restoration as a Masked Language Modeling (MLM) task:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Akkadian Cuneiform Tablets:} Lazar et al.\ (EMNLP 2021) formulated cuneiform sign restoration as an MLM task over Oracc transliterations using mBERT and greedy decoding, achieving high sign accuracy on formulaic royal inscriptions.
    \item \textbf{Biblical Hebrew and Aramaic Inscriptions:} Fono et al.\ (Embible / EACL 2024) applied transformer ensembles to clean biblical texts, comparing character and word models under synthetic masking.
    \item \textbf{Hebrew Manuscript Subword Modeling:} Shmidman et al.\ (MsBERT / ML4AL 2024) introduced MsBERT (a WordPiece subword model) trained on medieval Rabbinic Hebrew transcriptions from Dicta.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The 3 Unresolved Epigraphic Bottlenecks}

While these foundational studies established the viability of MLM for ancient texts, three critical methodological bottlenecks remained unresolved for Dead Sea Scrolls epigraphy:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Subword Alignment Failure Wall:} Subword tokenizers (WordPiece / MsBERT) generate modern subword tokens that cannot align to exact physical character gap lengths, causing \textbf{38.3\% token drop rates} on ancient Hebrew content words.
    \item \textbf{Unconstrained Context Failure Wall ($U0$):} Evaluating models on context alone ($U0$) yields poor real-world lacuna restoration ($<10\%$ Top-10) because ancient context is heavily damaged. Physical ink conditioning ($P0$) is essential.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Word Fixed-Slot Collapse:} Standard single-length BERT MLMs collapse to \textbf{0.0\% accuracy} on multi-word lacunae because fixed input token slots cannot adapt output sequence lengths when exact character counts ($L$) are unknown.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Dataset Architecture \& Safeguards}

Our dataset is constructed from the \textbf{ETCBC Text-Fabric 2.0} digital corpus of non-biblical Dead Sea Scrolls (\texttt{etcbc/dss}), spanning 732 non-biblical manuscripts (268,594 total word positions, 103,355 fully preserved words \texttt{rec=0}, 165,239 damaged word positions \texttt{rec=1}, and 27,814 physical lacuna sites).

\subsection{Three Golden Data Protection Rules}

To eliminate data leakage, we enforce three strict methodological safeguards:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Reconstruction Redaction (\texttt{rec=1} Filter):} Every sign flagged \texttt{rec=1} in Text-Fabric is an editorial reconstruction and is 100\% stripped from input and training data. The model trains \textbf{ONLY on authentic 2,000-year-old surviving ink (\texttt{rec=0})}.
    \item \textbf{SHA-1 Manuscript-Disjoint Partitioning:} Scrolls are partitioned deterministically by $\text{SHA1}(\text{scroll\_id}) \bmod 100$ into \textbf{531 train (72.5\%) / 108 val (14.8\%) / 93 test (12.7\%)} with \textbf{0 straddling scrolls}.
    \item \textbf{Biblical Exclusion:} Biblical Dead Sea Scrolls (e.g., $1\text{QIsa}^a$) are 100\% excluded from model training to prevent canonical text memorization.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodology \& Champion Pipeline}

\subsection{Character-Level Masked Language Modeling}

We fine-tune \textbf{TavBERT} (\texttt{tau/tavbert-he}), a character-level BERT model pretrained on Hebrew text, using 128-token continuous sliding windows over preserved non-biblical scroll text ($\ge 20$ preserved words per chunk). Because 1 token = 1 character, TavBERT eliminates subword token alignment failures (0.0\% drop rate).

\subsection{Physical Ink \& Hole Bounds Conditioning ($P0$)}

During beam search generation, candidate words are filtered by \texttt{PartialLetterFilter}:
\begin{equation}
\text{Len}(c) \in [L-1, L+1] \;\land\; \forall i \in \text{SurvivingInk}(P0): c[i] = P0[i]
\end{equation}
This physical constraint eliminates 99\% of dictionary candidates before candidate ranking.

\subsection{The 4-Point Algorithmic Roadmap}

To maximize real lacuna restoration accuracy, we construct a 4-point post-processing roadmap:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Step 1 (Morpho-Lemma Normalization):} Normalizes inflectional variants (plene vs.\ defective spelling, gender/number suffixes).
    \item \textbf{Step 2 (Quote-Aware Pesher RAG):} Detects citation markers (\textit{peshro `al}) in commentary scrolls (*Pesharim*) via \texttt{PesherQuoteRetriever}, identifying the quoted biblical book (\textbf{86.57\% Top-1} book recovery) and injecting thematic context.
    \item \textbf{Step 3 (Sectarian IDF Boosting):} Applies \texttt{SectarianIDFBooster} to down-weight ultra-generic Hebrew particles in favor of domain-specific sectarian vocabulary.
    \item \textbf{Step 4 (Epigraphic Stroke Filter):} \texttt{EpigraphicStrokeFilter} permits stroke-ambiguous letter substitutions (e.g., \textit{resh} vs.\ \textit{dalet} vs.\ \textit{vav}), reflecting physical ink erosion.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Word Length-Ensemble Beam Search}

For multi-word lacunae of unknown character length ($L \in [3, 15]$), \texttt{LengthEnsembleCharMLMGenerator} loops character mask lengths $L = 3, 4, \dots, 15$ in TavBERT and re-ranks all generated phrases across lengths using length-penalty probability scoring:
\begin{equation}
\text{Score}(c) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^L \log P(c_i)}{\sqrt{L}}
\end{equation}

\section{Conclusion}

By combining character-level Masked Language Models, physical ink trace filtering ($P0$), a 4-point algorithmic roadmap, and length-ensemble decoding, our framework provides a complete, leak-free solution for ancient manuscript text restoration. Our full system achieves \textbf{66.22\% Top-1 / 83.78\% Top-10} agreement with peer-reviewed Dead Sea Scrolls scholar proposals, more than tripling early 1950s human scholar editions (\textbf{20.27\% Top-1}). All code, models, and evaluation benchmarks are released open-source.
