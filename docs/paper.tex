\begin{abstract}
Restoring damaged text (\textit{lacunae}) in the 2,000-year-old Dead Sea Scrolls is a core challenge in digital humanities. Inspired by recent Masked Language Modeling (MLM) applications to ancient cuneiform and Semitic manuscripts, we present a unified epigraphic restoration framework for non-biblical Dead Sea Scrolls. We show that subword tokenizers (WordPiece) suffer a \textbf{38.3\% alignment failure rate} on character-bounded gaps, whereas character-level MLMs (TavBERT FT) eliminate alignment drops entirely (\textbf{0.0\% drops}). On real physical manuscript lacunae, conditioning on surviving ink traces ($P0$) and physical hole bounds ($L \pm 1$) lifts Top-10 accuracy from 9.46\% ($U0$ unconstrained) to 64.86\% ($P0$). Incorporating our 4-point algorithmic roadmap elevates performance to \textbf{66.22\% Top-1 / 83.78\% Top-10} on peer-reviewed Qumran-Digital scholar targets ($n=74$), more than tripling early 1950s human scholar editions (\textbf{20.27\% Top-1}). For unknown-length multi-word gaps, our length-ensemble beam search achieves \textbf{14.2\% Hit@10}, breaking the 0.0\% collapse wall of single-length MLMs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Dead Sea Scrolls—discovered in caves near Qumran between 1947 and 1956—comprise over 25,000 fragments of ancient Hebrew and Aramaic texts (3rd c.\ BCE -- 1st c.\ CE). Physical rot and parchment decay have left extensive holes (\textit{lacunae}) across these manuscripts, requiring editors to spend decades reconstructing lost wording by hand.

Recent research has applied Masked Language Modeling to ancient texts, including Akkadian cuneiform (\cite{lazar2021filling}), ancient Hebrew/Aramaic inscriptions (\cite{fono2024embible}), and medieval Hebrew manuscripts (\cite{shmidman2024msbert}). However, three bottlenecks remained:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Subword Alignment Drops:} Subword tokenizers (MsBERT) fail on exact character gap bounds, dropping 38.3\% of content words.
    \item \textbf{Unconstrained Context Collapse ($U0$):} Context-only models yield $<10\%$ Top-10 accuracy on heavily damaged scrolls.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Word Fixed-Slot Collapse:} Standard MLMs collapse to 0.0\% accuracy on multi-word lacunae of unknown character length.
\end{itemize}

\section{Dataset Architecture \& Safeguards}

Our corpus is built from ETCBC Text-Fabric 2.0 (\texttt{etcbc/dss}), covering 732 non-biblical Dead Sea Scrolls (268,594 word positions and 27,814 physical lacuna sites).

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{100\% Reconstruction Redaction:} All editorial reconstructions (\texttt{rec=1}) are stripped prior to training (\texttt{modern\_reconstruction\_text\_emitted: false}).
    \item \textbf{SHA-1 Manuscript-Disjoint Partitioning:} Scrolls are split deterministically by $\text{SHA1}(\text{scroll\_id}) \bmod 100$ into 531 train / 108 val / 93 test scrolls (\textbf{0 straddling scrolls}).
    \item \textbf{Biblical Exclusion:} Biblical scrolls are 100\% excluded from training to prevent canonical memorization.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology \& Champion Pipeline}

\subsection{Character TavBERT MLM \& $P0$ Ink Filtering}
We fine-tune character-level TavBERT (\texttt{tau/tavbert-he}) on preserved text. Candidate words are filtered by physical ink strokes ($P0$) and physical hole bounds ($L \pm 1$):
\begin{equation}
c \in \mathcal{V} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \text{Len}(c) \in [L-1, L+1] \;\land\; c \text{ matches } P0
\end{equation}

\subsection{The 4-Point Roadmap \& Length Ensemble}
Our post-processing roadmap combines: (1) Morpho-lemma normalization, (2) Quote-aware Pesher RAG (\texttt{PesherQuoteRetriever}, 86.57\% book recovery), (3) Sectarian IDF boosting, and (4) Epigraphic stroke filtering. For multi-word gaps ($L \in [3, 15]$), \texttt{LengthEnsembleCharMLMGenerator} ranks candidate sequences via length-normalized probability scoring:
\begin{equation}
\text{Score}(c) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^L \log P(c_i)}{\sqrt{L}}
\end{equation}

\section{Conclusion}

Our framework achieves \textbf{66.22\% Top-1 / 83.78\% Top-10} accuracy on peer-reviewed Dead Sea Scrolls scholar targets, tripling early human editions (\textbf{20.27\% Top-1}). All code and models are released open-source.
